BuyIn: nearest-neighbour forecasting on intraday bars

Simon Eberhart · live results Oct 20, 2023 to Jan 8, 2024

BuyIn forecasts a stock's next few hours by finding the past stretches that looked most like the last few days and averaging what happened next. I traded its signals live for about three months: 61 trades, almost all single options contracts. The account ended +$130.97 after fees (+$288.00 before). Fees took 54% of the gross, the calls made money and the puts did not, and most of the profit came in the first weeks. This is a short account of how it works and what the log does and doesn't show.

1Method

Matching

Prices come in as 5-minute bars. A window of recent bars (price and volume as two channels) is z-score normalized, so only its shape counts, then compared by Euclidean distance with every historical window that ends at the same time of day. The forecast is the average continuation of the 100 closest matches, rescaled to the current price.

forecast = mean( continuation(w) for w in nearest(window, history, k=100) ) × current_price

The search is split across every core with multiprocessing. For the bigger runs I built a 20-node cluster out of old computers, with custom network protocols between them.

Trading

The auto-trader only enters when the 2-, 3- and 4-day windows all forecast the same direction past a z-score threshold. Position size comes from the Kelly criterion. Orders go through the Interactive Brokers API, and a position closes on a contrary signal or after four hours, whichever comes first.

2The live test

61 trades from Oct 20, 2023 to Jan 8, 2024: 27 calls, 33 puts and one stock purchase that was an accident. Each trade cost $70 to $889 (about $358 on average), because the account was small. That matters more than anything else below: a typical trade earned about $4.72 before fees and paid $2.53 in them.

4What it shows, and what it doesn't

Fees were the story. The signal made $288.00 gross and the account paid $154.44 to collect it. Fees were about $2 a trade whatever the size, so a bigger account would pay a much smaller share. This one kept 45% of the gross.

The profit is lumpy. The average trade returned −0.04% after fees, with a standard deviation of 7.9%. The dollar profit came from a few larger winners: MSFT alone (12 trades) made +$209, more than the whole account. 61% of trades won, but the average loss was a little bigger than the average win.

Calls worked and puts didn't. Calls won 21 of 27 and made +$225; puts won 16 of 33 and lost $90. I can't tell from 61 trades whether the model is worse at calling drops or the market just didn't drop much.

It's the model plus me. 26 of the 61 trades have a note, and many of them are about me: selling late, trading on thin evidence, ignoring the signal. The log measures the model and my discretion together. It isn't a clean test of the model alone.

It's small and short. 61 trades, one regime, about eleven weeks, no benchmark. It shows the system worked end to end, from forecast to filled order. It isn't enough to put a number on the edge.
