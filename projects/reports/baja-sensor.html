A fuel gauge for our Baja car that never touches the fuel. It works by listening to the tank. The tank sits on rubber mounts, so it rings every time the car hits a bump, and the pitch of that ringing changes as the tank empties. I designed the boards and wrote the firmware. They're being fabbed now.

1How it works

Two small accelerometer boards do the listening. One sits right under the tank and the other sits on the frame about 6 inches away. The frame board only feels the car. The tank board feels the car plus the tank ringing. Subtract one from the other and the car cancels out, which leaves the tank. An FFT then finds the frequency it's ringing at, and that tells you how much fuel is left.

The boards talk over CAN, so you can add as many as you like. One of them is the master and passes everything on to the car's logging computer.

2The board

Four layers, 59 by 48 mm. This is the layout straight from the fab files. Drag it around, zoom in, flip it over and switch layers on and off.

3The schematic

An STM32F446 reads an MPU-6050 accelerometer over I²C. There are two CAN ports with a transceiver each. INTER is for talking to the other boards and EXT goes out to the car. Every CAN line runs through a filter and a protection diode. There's also a programming header and a few test pads.
