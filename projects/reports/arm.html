I've spent a bit over two years trying to drive a robot arm with one motor. I'm still building it. I don't have torque or cycle-time figures worth publishing yet. I'm planning to raise money for it at some point.

1Mechanism

A conventional robot arm puts a motor at each joint. I put one motor on a shared drive shaft, then use clutches to connect that shaft to whichever joint needs to move. Engage a clutch, drive the motor, disengage it, and select another axis.

The joints can be addressed separately, but they can't all demand independent torque at the same time. Motion has to be scheduled around the one motor. That's a substantial limitation, although for some kinds of manipulation it may be worth trading simultaneous motion for fewer actuators.

I initially thought having one motor would make the arm much simpler. In practice, I just moved most of the difficulty into the clutches, which have to engage and release cleanly every time.

2Clutches

The first design used hobby servos to slide the clutches into place. It was a good way to test the idea. Next I designed my own solenoids, including the coil and magnet arrangement. I learned a lot about electromagnets. The newer versions use off-the-shelf solenoids to engage the clutches. Some of this work led to provisional patent filings.

4Next

Originally I was designing the whole arm around this transmission. That meant building a robot just to test one mechanism, which got old. I'm now turning it into an end effector for Universal Robots arms instead. The robot handles positioning and my drive sits on the end of it.

I'm planning to raise money for it at some point. I don't have performance numbers yet, so I'm not going to make any up.
